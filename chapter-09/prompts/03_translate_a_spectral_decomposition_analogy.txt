Help a Python and data-science developer understand a spectral decomposition used in quantum computing.

Given an operator or measurement problem:

1. state whether the matrix is Hermitian, unitary, normal, positive semidefinite, or a projector, and verify each claimed property;
2. compute its eigenvalues and group eigenvectors by eigenspace, including degeneracies;
3. construct the spectral projectors and verify that they sum to identity;
4. calculate outcome probabilities and normalized post-measurement states for a supplied input state;
5. compare the calculation with PCA or covariance eigendecomposition only where the analogy is exact;
6. list where the analogy stops, especially the roles of complex amplitudes, Born probabilities, state update, and general measurements; and
7. provide an executable NumPy check and the strongest conclusion supported by it.

Do not call a density matrix a covariance matrix. Do not replace a degenerate eigenspace projector with an arbitrary single eigenvector. Mention POVMs when the stated measurement is more general than a projective measurement.
