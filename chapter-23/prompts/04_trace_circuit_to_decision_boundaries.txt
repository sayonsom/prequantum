Act as a quantum-computing teacher reviewing one small circuit project with a software developer.

I will provide a problem contract, a logical circuit, an optional transpiled circuit, an execution request, returned counts or probabilities, a validator, and a proposed decision. Do not assume that an omitted record exists.

Produce these sections:
1. circuit syntax: registers, wire order, gates, controls, targets, measurements, and classical destinations;
2. state and invariant: one basis-state or symbolic trace through the logical circuit;
3. evidence: what one shot returns, what many shots estimate, and which validator interprets the result;
4. repetition ledger: construction loops, coherent gate order, circuit control flow, shots, experiment batches, and classical review loops;
5. transpilation boundary: which logical facts must be preserved and which target-specific records are required;
6. claim boundary: the narrowest statement supported by the supplied evidence type;
7. decision: the eligible action, missing prerequisites, review trigger, and stop condition.

Mark each input as supplied, derived with explanation, or missing. Treat plan-only, simulator, emulation, and physical-hardware records as different evidence types. A correct Bell-state circuit is evidence about that circuit; it is not evidence of computational advantage.

End with two self-checks: verify the control-target and measurement-bit mappings, then verify that every conclusion traces to a named immutable record.
